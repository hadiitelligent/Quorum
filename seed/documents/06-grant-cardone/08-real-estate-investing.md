# H. Real Estate Investing — 13 rules

## GC-H01 — Income-producing assets only

Rule: Cash-producing property beats appreciation speculation; the offering circulars target stabilised, income-producing multifamily.

In practice: Prioritise credible current or near-term positive cash flow.

Counterweight: Value-add may tolerate lower early cash flow when the repositioning thesis and capex are explicit.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources S24, S25, S26

## GC-H02 — Institutional scale: 200–600 units, Class A/B+

Rule: The documented strategy is large Class A garden/mid-rise multifamily, 2010s vintage; 'buying small' is mistake #1.

In practice: Compare 200+ unit opportunities against smaller assets on management efficiency and financing; avoid assets too small for on-site professional management.

Counterweight: Do not force unit count where local economics favour another format; translate 'units' to keys/sq ft per sector.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E16, S25, E15

## GC-H03 — Markets: inflow, landlord-neutral regulation, favourable tax

Rule: Florida/Texas/Sun Belt for migration, no state income tax and regulation; explicitly avoids California and New York.

In practice: Score employment, demographics, supply, demand, regulation and tax; translate to the local equivalent (e.g., provincial rent-control regime, land-transfer and foreign-buyer taxes).

Counterweight: A great location does not justify any price.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E15, S25, S26

## GC-H04 — Price off replacement cost, not last trade

Rule: 2023–24 buys marketed at '40% below replacement cost'.

In practice: Every acquisition paper shows purchase basis vs replacement cost and vs seller's basis.

Counterweight: Replacement cost is not a complete valuation method.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E18, E10, S26

## GC-H05 — Hunt maturing debt

Rule: The source's five-step process: locate assets → identify owners → find loan maturity dates → raise equity → secure debt. Orchid Run (2026) fits exactly.

In practice: Maintain a rolling list of assets in target markets with loan maturities inside 24 months; that is the acquisition pipeline.

Counterweight: Not every distressed seller is cheap.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E18, E14

## GC-H06 — Scale arithmetic: small increments × the whole book × the hold

Rule: 'A $25 rent increase into a $750,000 payday — it's called scale.'

In practice: Convert every operating decision into per-unit/month × units × 10-year hold.

Counterweight: NOI assumptions must respect market and tenant constraints.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E15, S24

## GC-H07 — Underwrite occupancy quality, not headline occupancy

Rule: Circulars set occupancy thresholds; the 2020 concession (15 months for 12) shows what headline occupancy hides.

In practice: Stress rent roll, concessions, delinquency and renewals, not the occupancy percentage alone.

Counterweight: Lease-up deals require separate treatment.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources S25, E8

## GC-H08 — Controllable NOI growth over cap-rate hope

Rule: Operational improvement is preferable to relying on cap-rate compression.

In practice: Identify rent, expense, ancillary revenue and management levers with owners and dates.

Counterweight: None.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S24, S26

## GC-H09 — Hold for generations; underwrite 10-year cycles

Rule: 'When I buy in 2023 I'm thinking about 2033, 2043, 2053'; circulars contemplate 7–10-year holds.

In practice: Underwrite cycles, debt maturities and capex over 10 years.

Counterweight: Exit earlier only per GC-B12.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E17, S24

## GC-H10 — Preserve multiple exits; model the no-refi case

Rule: Assets should support refinance, sale or continued hold.

In practice: Model all three before acquisition; the no-refinance case must not breach GC-F03.

Counterweight: Do not rely on refinancing being available.

Basis GOV · evidence C · sources S24, S25

## GC-H11 — Independent valuation on anything that drives fees or NAV [FIXED GUARDRAIL]

Rule: The source's fund NAVs were marked by the manager's own direct-cap appraisals; NAV rose while cash fell.

In practice: An independent valuer marks any asset whose value drives fees, NAV, covenants or distributions; the board sees the mark and the method.

Counterweight: None — fixed.

Basis DOES · evidence A · sources E3, E4

## GC-H12 — Property-manager oversight is the owner's liability [FIXED GUARDRAIL]

Rule: Wellington Club: workforce-housing overcharges 2018–21; the company said it 'relies on the property manager'.

In practice: Annual compliance audit of every third-party manager (rent regulation, affordable/workforce obligations, deposits); KPIs, reporting and budget/leasing/capex sign-off retained by the owner.

Counterweight: None — fixed.

Basis DOES · evidence A · sources E25, S24

## GC-H13 — Insurance, climate and capex in the shock case

Rule: Florida concentration makes hurricane/insurance cost a primary risk that the source's public statements never mention.

In practice: The −20%/+200bp shock case includes insurance repricing, climate capex and deferred-maintenance catch-up.

Counterweight: None.

Basis GOV · evidence C · sources E4, S26
