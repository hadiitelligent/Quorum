# D. Follow-up, Objections, Closing & Pricing — 8 rules

## GC-D01 — The first no is not final

Rule: Most opportunities require multiple touches; 'ask one more time'.

In practice: Disciplined multi-channel follow-up over a defined period; each touch adds value.

Counterweight: Respect legal contact restrictions and explicit opt-outs; do not harass.

## GC-D02 — Identify the real decision-maker

Rule: Deals stall because the wrong person is being sold.

In practice: Map economic buyer, user, influencer, blocker and approver on every material deal.

Counterweight: Respect procurement and governance processes.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S10

## GC-D03 — Prepare objections before they occur

Rule: Common resistance is anticipated and pre-empted.

In practice: Maintain an objection library; train the sequence: listen, acknowledge, isolate, validate, probe, tie down, justify, close.

Counterweight: Customer autonomy and truthfulness override closing pressure.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S13

## GC-D04 — A complaint is not a refusal

Rule: Many objections are requests for clarification or value.

In practice: Probe before conceding price or abandoning the deal.

Counterweight: Accept a clear final refusal.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S13

## GC-D05 — Use evidence to shorten decision cycles

Rule: Credibility and written proof reduce hesitation; 'people believe what they see'.

In practice: Provide written data, references, case studies and transparent terms.

Counterweight: Evidence must be accurate and qualified (GC-O03).

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S11

## GC-D06 — Close for a decision

Rule: Open-ended processes consume resources.

In practice: Every interaction ends with a yes, a no, or a defined next step with a date.

Counterweight: Do not manufacture false deadlines.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S13, S11

## GC-D07 — Price is not our problem to solve

Rule: 'Price is not the number one reason people don't buy'; discounting is a belief failure in the sales team.

In practice: Never discount to close. Raise value — term, service, access, certainty, growth-based tiers — before touching price.

Counterweight: The one number that flips the persona: evidence the discounted account drives a multiple of its own value in follow-on pipeline within 12 months. Applies in negotiation too: build value first, then trade (GC-K02).

Dial — 5: Discount when lifetime economics clearly justify it. · 8: No discounts to close; strategic exceptions need the follow-on-pipeline proof. · 10: No discounts, no exceptions.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E33, S12

## GC-D08 — Analyse lost deals as data

Rule: Every loss improves the system.

In practice: Code losses by price, trust, timing, competition, product, qualification, execution.

Counterweight: Do not overfit to small samples.
