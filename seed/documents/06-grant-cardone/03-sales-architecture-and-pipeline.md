# C. Sales Architecture & Pipeline — 11 rules

## GC-C01 — Sales is survival infrastructure

Rule: Revenue generation is a company-wide priority; every problem is a sales or marketing problem until proven otherwise.

In practice: Board packs include pipeline, conversion, average ticket, cycle time and retention every meeting.

Counterweight: Do not pressure customers into unsuitable purchases.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S8, S9

## GC-C02 — Top line before cost line

Rule: Before cutting cost, establish whether inadequate revenue is the root problem.

In practice: When margin falls, model revenue-side fixes alongside expense actions; present both.

Counterweight: Unproductive cost still requires discipline.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S9, E36

## GC-C03 — Sales is a documented operating system, not improvisation

Rule: Stages from prospecting to qualification, presentation, objection, close and follow-up are defined and trained.

In practice: Require a written sales process with stage definitions and exit criteria.

Counterweight: Allow adaptation for customer context.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S13, S14

## GC-C04 — Selling, negotiating and closing are distinct skills

Rule: Train and measure each separately.

In practice: Diagnose lost deals by which skill failed.

Counterweight: Roles may combine in small teams; the skills remain distinct.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S12

## GC-C05 — Train constantly

Rule: Sales skill decays without repetition; the source's organisations train daily.

In practice: Standing training cadence with call reviews, objection drills and manager coaching, tied to measured outcomes.

Counterweight: Training must connect to outcomes, not attendance.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S14, S15

## GC-C06 — Managers are multipliers

Rule: A manager produces through the team, not by rescuing every deal.

In practice: Define manager KPIs, one-on-one cadence, coaching load and hiring responsibility.

Counterweight: Managers enter strategic deals when their involvement adds leverage.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S14

## GC-C07 — Measure pipeline before month-end

Rule: Leading indicators beat post-period explanations.

In practice: Track activity and pipeline sufficiency early enough to intervene in-period.

Counterweight: Avoid incentivising meaningless activity metrics.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S14, S17

## GC-C08 — One scorecard, few KPIs, visible to all

Rule: Visible metrics reduce ambiguity.

In practice: Role-specific KPIs with targets, owners and review cadence; few enough to influence behaviour.

Counterweight: Avoid public humiliation as a KPI tool (GC-I05).

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources S14, S15, E30

## GC-C09 — Sell the problem solved

Rule: Buyers purchase outcomes; frame every offer around the customer's economic or emotional problem.

In practice: Rewrite offers in outcome language before launch.

Counterweight: Never fabricate a problem or outcome.

Basis SAYS · evidence A · sources S29, S15

## GC-C10 — Size sales capacity to the target

Rule: Revenue targets must be supported by trained capacity.

In practice: Model leads per rep, close rate, ramp time and management span against the target.

Counterweight: Hiring without lead flow destroys productivity.

Basis SAYS · evidence B · sources S14, S17

## GC-C11 — Pay for output; overpay top performers; sales comp uncapped

Rule: 'They only eat what they kill.' 'OVERPAY your employees' (2022). Pay for output in every role; top-quartile producers are paid above market.

In practice: Variable comp is the majority of top-quartile pay in revenue roles; no caps on commission; pay tied to measurable economic outcomes with guardrails.

Counterweight: Do not incentivise behaviour that damages customers or long-term value (GC-I08).

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E31, E32, S14
