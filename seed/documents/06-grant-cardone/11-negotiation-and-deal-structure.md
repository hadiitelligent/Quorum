# K. Negotiation & Deal Structure — 7 rules

## GC-K02 — Trade, don't give

Rule: Every concession seeks reciprocal value.

In practice: Exchange price for term, volume, speed, deposit, exclusivity or reduced risk.

Counterweight: Small goodwill concessions can be rational.

Basis SAYS · evidence B · sources S13

## GC-K03 — Prepare the ladder and the walk-away

Rule: Aggression requires boundaries.

In practice: Concession ladders, walk-away points, maximum price, minimum return and liquidity limits are set before negotiation starts.

Counterweight: Revisit boundaries only with new information; never misrepresent authority or alternatives.

Basis GOV · evidence C · sources S13, S17

## GC-K04 — Reframe stalled deals structurally

Rule: A bad price can become a good deal under better terms.

In practice: Test seller financing, earn-outs, contingencies, staged closes, different scope.

Counterweight: Structure cannot rescue bad economics.

Basis SAYS · evidence B · sources S20, S19

## GC-K05 — Certainty and speed are negotiating assets

Rule: $100M of commitments in 72 hours; three ~$150M closes in six weeks.

In practice: Offer credible financing, diligence readiness and clear closing mechanics; use speed to win price.

Counterweight: Never promise certainty you cannot deliver.

Basis BOTH · evidence A · sources E11, E10, S19

## GC-K06 — Negotiate total economics

Rule: Headline price is one variable.

In practice: Model taxes, working capital, warranties, financing, timing, contingencies.

Counterweight: Keep complexity proportional to deal size.

Basis SAYS · evidence B · sources S19, S20

## GC-K07 — Preserve the relationship where repeat business matters

Rule: A win that destroys the counterparty reduces long-term value.

In practice: Optimise repeatability in strategic relationships; treat brokers, lenders and sellers as pipeline.

Counterweight: Do not accept bad economics to be liked.

Basis SAYS · evidence B · sources S11, S17

## GC-K08 — Senior involvement is selective

Rule: Executive presence unlocks strategic deals when routine involvement would cheapen it.

In practice: Escalate when authority, trust or creativity can change the outcome.

Counterweight: Avoid undermining account ownership.

Basis SAYS · evidence B · sources S10, S14
