# Marketing, Attention & Distribution — 10 principles

## GC-047 — Attention precedes monetization

Rule: Being unknown is a strategic disadvantage.

In practice: Invest in qualified awareness before assuming the market will discover the product.

Counterweight: Attention that damages trust can destroy value.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S6, S7

## GC-048 — Best-known can beat best-product

Rule: Distribution and familiarity materially affect outcomes.

In practice: Score brand awareness and channel access alongside product quality.

Counterweight: Do not use this to excuse inferior products.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S6

## GC-049 — Promote consistently

Rule: Marketing frequency should be persistent enough to maintain top-of-mind presence.

In practice: Establish content and outreach cadences across relevant channels.

Counterweight: Optimize frequency against audience fatigue and channel economics.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S6, S7

## GC-050 — Use multiple channels

Rule: Dependence on one distribution platform creates fragility.

In practice: Build a portfolio of owned, earned, paid and partner channels.

Counterweight: Avoid spreading resources across channels that cannot be executed well.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S7, S17

## GC-051 — Control the narrative where possible

Rule: Active communication helps shape reputation.

In practice: Respond to customer concerns, publish evidence and communicate company value directly.

Counterweight: Never suppress legitimate criticism through deception or intimidation.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S7, S11

## GC-052 — Measure marketing by business outcomes

Rule: Visibility is useful when it creates pipeline, revenue or strategic access.

In practice: Track qualified leads, CAC, conversion, revenue and payback by channel.

Counterweight: Some brand investment requires longer measurement horizons.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S17

## GC-053 — Turn expertise into media

Rule: Educational content can create authority and demand.

In practice: Publish useful problem-solving material that demonstrates competence.

Counterweight: Claims must be substantiated.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S7, S11

## GC-054 — Make calls to action explicit

Rule: Attention should have a next step.

In practice: Every campaign should define the desired action and measurement.

Counterweight: Avoid deceptive interface or pressure tactics.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S7

## GC-055 — Expect criticism at scale

Rule: Greater visibility increases negative feedback.

In practice: Distinguish signal from noise; adjust when criticism identifies real customer or legal risk.

Counterweight: Do not treat all criticism as proof of success.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S6

## GC-056 — Reputation is distribution infrastructure

Rule: Trust amplifies marketing efficiency.

In practice: Protect credibility because distrust increases friction and acquisition cost.

Counterweight: Short-term attention cannot justify long-term trust destruction.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S11, S27
