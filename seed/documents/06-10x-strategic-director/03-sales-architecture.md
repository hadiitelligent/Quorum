# Sales Architecture — 12 principles

## GC-025 — Treat sales as survival infrastructure

Rule: Revenue generation is a company-wide strategic priority.

In practice: Board reviews should include pipeline, conversion, average ticket, cycle time and retention.

Counterweight: Do not pressure customers into unsuitable purchases.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S8, S9

## GC-026 — Top line deserves early attention

Rule: Before cutting cost, investigate whether inadequate revenue is the root problem.

In practice: When margin falls, model revenue-side fixes alongside expense actions.

Counterweight: Unproductive costs still require discipline.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S9

## GC-027 — Build a defined sales process

Rule: Sales should be a repeatable operating system rather than individual improvisation.

In practice: Document stages from prospecting to qualification, presentation, objections, close and follow-up.

Counterweight: Allow adaptation for customer context.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S13, S14

## GC-028 — Separate selling, negotiating and closing

Rule: These are distinct capabilities.

In practice: Train and measure each function separately rather than treating all sales work as one skill.

Counterweight: Roles may be combined in small teams, but the skills remain distinct.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S12

## GC-029 — Train continuously

Rule: Sales skill degrades without repetition and coaching.

In practice: Use frequent drills, call reviews, objection practice and manager coaching.

Counterweight: Training must connect to measured outcomes.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S15

## GC-030 — Make managers multipliers

Rule: A manager's job is to produce through the team, not rescue every deal personally.

In practice: Define manager KPIs, one-on-ones, coaching cadence and hiring responsibility.

Counterweight: Managers can enter strategic deals when their involvement adds leverage.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14

## GC-031 — Measure pipeline before month-end

Rule: Leading indicators matter more than post-period explanations.

In practice: Track activity and pipeline sufficiency early enough to intervene.

Counterweight: Avoid incentivizing meaningless activity metrics.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-032 — Use a common scorecard

Rule: Visible metrics reduce ambiguity.

In practice: Create role-specific KPIs with targets, owners and review cadence.

Counterweight: KPIs should be few enough to influence behavior.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S15, S17

## GC-033 — Raise value before reducing price

Rule: A price objection often signals insufficient perceived value or trust.

In practice: Strengthen the case, evidence, terms or solution before discounting.

Counterweight: Discount when economically strategic, not reflexively.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S12, S11

## GC-034 — Sell the problem solved

Rule: Product knowledge matters because buyers purchase outcomes.

In practice: Frame offers around the customer's economic or emotional problem.

Counterweight: Never fabricate a problem or outcome.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S29, S15

## GC-035 — Use sales capacity deliberately

Rule: Revenue targets must be supported by enough trained capacity.

In practice: Model leads per rep, close rate, ramp time and management span.

Counterweight: Hiring without adequate lead flow can destroy productivity.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-036 — Make revenue predictable

Rule: A strong business should know how leads become revenue.

In practice: Build repeatable acquisition, conversion and retention systems.

Counterweight: Some project businesses remain lumpy; use weighted pipeline models.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S16
