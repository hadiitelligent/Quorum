# Negotiation & Deal Structure — 10 principles

## GC-103 — Do not reduce price before building value

Rule: Price concessions should not be the first response.

In practice: Clarify value, outcomes and alternatives before discounting.

Counterweight: Use price strategically when it improves lifetime economics.

## GC-104 — Separate negotiation from closing

Rule: Negotiation changes terms; closing obtains commitment.

In practice: Know which problem is being solved before changing the offer.

Counterweight: In practice they may occur in the same conversation.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S12

## GC-105 — Prepare objections and counters

Rule: Negotiation improves with preplanned responses.

In practice: Create concession ladders, walk-away points and alternative structures.

Counterweight: Never misrepresent authority or alternatives.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S13

## GC-106 — Trade, don't give

Rule: Every concession should seek reciprocal value.

In practice: Exchange price for term, volume, speed, deposit, exclusivity or reduced risk.

Counterweight: Small goodwill concessions can be rational.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S13

## GC-107 — Reframe stalled deals structurally

Rule: A bad price may become a good deal under better terms.

In practice: Test seller financing, earnouts, contingencies, staged closes or different scope.

Counterweight: Structure cannot rescue fundamentally bad economics.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S20, S19

## GC-108 — Know your walk-away conditions

Rule: Aggression requires boundaries.

In practice: Define maximum price, minimum return, legal requirements and liquidity limits before negotiating.

Counterweight: Revisit boundaries only with new information.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S18

## GC-109 — Increase certainty as a negotiating asset

Rule: Speed and execution confidence can improve terms.

In practice: Offer credible financing, diligence readiness and clear closing mechanics.

Counterweight: Never promise certainty you cannot deliver.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-110 — Negotiate total economics

Rule: Headline price is only one variable.

In practice: Model taxes, working capital, warranties, financing, timing and contingencies.

Counterweight: Keep complexity proportional to deal size.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-111 — Preserve relationship where repeat business matters

Rule: A win that destroys the counterparty can reduce long-term value.

In practice: Optimize repeatability and reputation in strategic relationships.

Counterweight: Do not accept structurally bad economics merely to be liked.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S11, S17

## GC-112 — Use senior involvement selectively

Rule: Executive presence can unlock strategic deals.

In practice: Escalate when authority, trust or creativity can change the outcome.

Counterweight: Avoid making senior intervention routine.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S10, S14
