# Trust, Reputation & Customer Value — 10 principles

## GC-133 — Trust is an economic asset

Rule: Distrust lengthens sales cycles and raises acquisition cost.

In practice: Track reputation, reviews, complaints, churn and referral behavior.

Counterweight: Do not reduce trust to a marketing metric only.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S11

## GC-134 — Back claims with evidence

Rule: Credibility rises when assertions are documented.

In practice: Use data, contracts, case studies and third-party proof.

Counterweight: Qualify projections and uncertainty.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S11

## GC-135 — Own customer skepticism

Rule: The seller must reduce reasonable uncertainty.

In practice: Address terms, risk, product fit and proof proactively.

Counterweight: Some buyers will rationally decline; respect that.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S11

## GC-136 — Do not let aggressive selling create legal exposure

Rule: Promotional intensity must stay inside disclosure and securities rules.

In practice: For financial/investment promotions, require legal review and consistency with offering documents.

Counterweight: This is a governance guardrail derived partly from active litigation, not a finding of wrongdoing.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S27, S26

## GC-137 — Protect long-term brand over short-term conversion

Rule: A one-time close can be negative if it creates churn, complaints or regulatory risk.

In practice: Include lifetime value and reputation in sales incentives.

Counterweight: Not all complaints indicate misconduct.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S11, S27

## GC-138 — Respond visibly to service failures

Rule: Trust can be rebuilt through ownership and resolution.

In practice: Create escalation and remediation protocols.

Counterweight: Do not admit legal liability without counsel where material.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S11, S17

## GC-139 — Use transparency to accelerate decisions

Rule: Clear terms reduce uncertainty.

In practice: Present costs, obligations, risks and expected outcomes in understandable form.

Counterweight: Complex regulated offerings require professional disclosure.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S11, S26

## GC-140 — Customer value must exceed price

Rule: Sustainable selling requires perceived and actual value.

In practice: Improve outcomes, convenience, certainty and service before relying on discounting.

Counterweight: Measure whether customers actually realize value.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S12

## GC-141 — Reputation risk deserves board treatment

Rule: Brand damage can impair capital access and enterprise value.

In practice: Include reputation assessment in major launches, financing and acquisitions.

Counterweight: Avoid paralysis from fear of criticism.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S6, S27

## GC-142 — Differentiate criticism from evidence

Rule: Public criticism is not automatically proof of strategic error.

In practice: Investigate whether complaints reveal recurring operational, legal or ethical problems.

Counterweight: Do not dismiss critics merely as 'haters.'

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S6, S27
