# Time, Focus & Productivity — 10 principles

## GC-123 — Make time according to priorities

Rule: Time allocation should reflect declared goals.

In practice: Audit calendars against strategic priorities.

Counterweight: Protect recovery and health required for sustained performance.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S5

## GC-124 — Track time before claiming scarcity

Rule: Many time constraints are allocation problems.

In practice: Use periodic time studies for leaders and key roles.

Counterweight: Some jobs genuinely face capacity limits.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S5

## GC-125 — Convert time through delegation

Rule: Hiring and systems can multiply executive output.

In practice: Delegate repeatable work when the economics justify it.

Counterweight: Do not delegate accountability for critical judgments blindly.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S5, S17

## GC-126 — Compress cycle times

Rule: Faster productive throughput creates strategic capacity.

In practice: Measure sales cycles, decision times, project lead times and approval delays.

Counterweight: Speed must not sacrifice safety or diligence.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S5, S30

## GC-127 — Protect high-value work

Rule: Executive time should concentrate on decisions with disproportionate impact.

In practice: Identify the few activities that most influence revenue, capital and people.

Counterweight: Maintain enough operational contact to avoid detachment.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S5, S30

## GC-128 — Align stakeholders on priorities

Rule: Conflicting agendas waste time.

In practice: Make top priorities explicit across leadership and teams.

Counterweight: Allow constructive dissent on the priorities themselves.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S5, S17

## GC-129 — Schedule priorities rather than hope for them

Rule: Important work should have calendar ownership.

In practice: Reserve time blocks for strategic selling, hiring, capital allocation and review.

Counterweight: Remain flexible for genuine emergencies.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S5

## GC-130 — Eliminate low-return activity

Rule: Busy is not the same as productive.

In practice: Stop meetings, reports and projects without strategic contribution.

Counterweight: Retain necessary compliance and maintenance work.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S5, S17

## GC-131 — Use challenges to raise throughput

Rule: Stretch targets can expose wasted capacity.

In practice: Run time-boxed productivity experiments with quality controls.

Counterweight: Do not normalize unsustainable workload.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S5, S3

## GC-132 — Review priorities as conditions change

Rule: Time allocation must follow the strategy.

In practice: Rebalance calendars after acquisitions, crises or leadership changes.

Counterweight: Avoid constant reprioritization that destroys focus.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S5
