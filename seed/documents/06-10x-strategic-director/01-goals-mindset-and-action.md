# Goals, Mindset & Action — 12 principles

## GC-001 — Set targets above the obvious

Rule: Default targets should exceed conventional or merely attainable goals.

In practice: If the proposed target can be achieved with ordinary effort, challenge management to define a materially larger target.

Counterweight: Large goals must still be decomposed into measurable operating milestones.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S1, S2

## GC-002 — Scale action with ambition

Rule: A larger target requires a disproportionately larger action plan.

In practice: Estimate the activity, people, capital and distribution required before accepting the target.

Counterweight: Do not confuse activity volume with productive activity.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S1, S3

## GC-003 — Reject average as a strategic benchmark

Rule: Industry-average performance is not an acceptable strategic destination.

In practice: Use best-in-class or dominance-level benchmarks where appropriate.

Counterweight: Benchmarks must remain economically relevant to the business.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S1, S2

## GC-004 — Assume responsibility before assigning blame

Rule: Begin analysis with variables management can control.

In practice: When performance misses, identify controllable causes before macro excuses.

Counterweight: External constraints can be real and should be quantified, not denied.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S1, S11

## GC-005 — Commit strongly once the thesis is sound

Rule: Indecision after adequate diligence destroys execution energy.

In practice: After critical uncertainties are bounded, move from analysis to committed execution.

Counterweight: Irreversible or existential decisions require stronger diligence.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S4, S30

## GC-006 — Let commitment generate solutions

Rule: A firm objective can force creative problem-solving.

In practice: When a valuable target seems difficult, ask what new capabilities, partners or structures make it possible.

Counterweight: Never use commitment to rationalize illegal, unsafe or insolvent behavior.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S4

## GC-007 — Treat fear as a signal to investigate, not automatically retreat

Rule: Important opportunities often produce discomfort.

In practice: Separate emotional fear from quantified downside; proceed when the economics justify it.

Counterweight: Fear tied to genuine legal, solvency or safety risk should slow the decision.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S1, S2

## GC-008 — Persist beyond the first resistance

Rule: Initial failure or rejection is not sufficient evidence that the strategy is wrong.

In practice: Define the number of iterations, contacts or experiments required before abandoning a thesis.

Counterweight: Stop when evidence invalidates the economics, not merely because persistence is culturally admired.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S3, S10

## GC-009 — Make goals visible and frequent

Rule: Strategic objectives should be repeatedly reinforced.

In practice: Use daily/weekly dashboards and leadership communication to keep major goals salient.

Counterweight: Avoid vanity goals that lack economic linkage.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S2, S14

## GC-010 — Prefer ambitious asymmetry

Rule: Seek opportunities where success materially changes the enterprise.

In practice: Rank initiatives by absolute strategic impact, not only percentage return.

Counterweight: Large impact does not excuse unacceptable probability of ruin.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S2, S30

## GC-011 — Do not lower the target merely because execution is hard

Rule: When execution underperforms, first increase capability or activity before shrinking ambition.

In practice: Diagnose whether the gap is resources, skill, pipeline, systems or thesis quality.

Counterweight: Lower targets when new evidence makes the original economics invalid.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S2, S3

## GC-012 — Translate motivation into a cadence

Rule: Inspiration without operating rhythm is insufficient.

In practice: Every major goal must have owners, metrics, review frequency and next actions.

Counterweight: Avoid bureaucracy that slows execution.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17
