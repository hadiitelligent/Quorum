# Leadership, People & Accountability — 12 principles

## GC-081 — Performance is execution, not intention

Rule: Results and measurable behaviors matter more than effort claims.

In practice: Define benchmarks and review actual output.

Counterweight: Account for role complexity and leading indicators.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S15

## GC-082 — Give direct feedback

Rule: Ambiguity permits underperformance to persist.

In practice: Managers should state the gap, required standard and deadline clearly.

Counterweight: Directness should remain professional and factual.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S15, S14

## GC-083 — Build leaders who multiply people

Rule: Leadership should increase output through others.

In practice: Promote and train managers for coaching, hiring, accountability and systems.

Counterweight: Top individual performance alone does not prove management skill.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14

## GC-084 — Hire for capacity to produce

Rule: Recruiting should predict performance, not merely fill seats.

In practice: Use structured scorecards, role outcomes and evidence of past performance.

Counterweight: Avoid discriminatory or irrelevant criteria.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-085 — Train new hires deliberately

Rule: Ramp should be designed, not improvised.

In practice: Use structured onboarding, training milestones and early performance checks.

Counterweight: Customize for role complexity.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-086 — Hold one-on-ones with purpose

Rule: Manager attention should identify obstacles and commitments.

In practice: Use a repeatable agenda: metrics, pipeline/work, obstacles, development, commitments.

Counterweight: Avoid meetings without decisions or follow-up.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14

## GC-087 — Make accountability visible

Rule: Teams perform better when standards are explicit and measured.

In practice: Use dashboards and clear ownership.

Counterweight: Avoid public humiliation or manipulative pressure.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S15

## GC-088 — Address chronic underperformance

Rule: Persistent failure against fair standards is a management issue.

In practice: Coach, resource, reassign or exit based on evidence.

Counterweight: Ensure expectations, tools and feedback were adequate first.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S15

## GC-089 — Build a bench before growth demands it

Rule: Scale requires leaders ahead of capacity constraints.

In practice: Identify successor and second-level leaders in critical functions.

Counterweight: Do not overhire layers before economics support them.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S17

## GC-090 — Remove founder bottlenecks

Rule: A business dependent on one person cannot scale reliably.

In practice: Document decisions, delegate authority and build management systems.

Counterweight: Founder involvement can remain valuable in capital allocation, culture and key relationships.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S19

## GC-091 — Align incentives with enterprise outcomes

Rule: Compensation should reinforce desired production and quality.

In practice: Tie variable pay to measurable economic outcomes and guardrails.

Counterweight: Do not incentivize behavior that damages customers or long-term value.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-092 — Culture should reward action and accountability

Rule: Operating culture must reinforce standards.

In practice: Celebrate production, learning, ownership and improvement.

Counterweight: Do not glorify burnout or unethical behavior.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S15, S17
