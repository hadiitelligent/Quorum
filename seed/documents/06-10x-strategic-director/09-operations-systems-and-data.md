# Operations, Systems & Data — 10 principles

## GC-093 — Systems beat heroics

Rule: A scalable company cannot depend on exceptional improvisation.

In practice: Document repeatable workflows and decision rights.

Counterweight: Keep systems lightweight enough for speed.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-094 — Standardize before scaling

Rule: Growth should replicate a working unit.

In practice: Define the core process, economics and controls before multiplying locations/teams.

Counterweight: Some experimentation must occur before standardization.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S20

## GC-095 — Know the numbers

Rule: Decisions should be data-informed.

In practice: Maintain timely P&L, cash, pipeline, unit economics and operational KPIs.

Counterweight: Numbers require context and quality checks.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S19

## GC-096 — Use leading indicators

Rule: Lagging financials arrive too late for intervention.

In practice: Track activity, pipeline, utilization, hiring funnel, backlog and cycle times.

Counterweight: Avoid metrics that can be gamed without improving outcomes.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-097 — Create clear functional ownership

Rule: Every critical result needs an accountable owner.

In practice: Assign one executive or role to each metric/process.

Counterweight: Cross-functional work still requires collaboration.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S19

## GC-098 — Design for founder independence

Rule: The business should increasingly operate without constant founder intervention.

In practice: Build processes, management authority and reporting cadence.

Counterweight: Do not remove founder judgment where it remains a unique advantage.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S17

## GC-099 — Use technology as leverage

Rule: Technology should accelerate scale, consistency and information flow.

In practice: Prioritize automation where it removes bottlenecks or improves customer economics.

Counterweight: Avoid technology projects without measurable business value.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S17

## GC-100 — Integrate growth with operations

Rule: Sales growth that operations cannot fulfill destroys trust.

In practice: Before scaling demand, verify capacity, service levels and working capital.

Counterweight: Temporary strain can be accepted with a credible ramp plan.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S16, S17

## GC-101 — Build an operating cadence

Rule: Execution improves with structured review cycles.

In practice: Use daily/weekly/monthly rhythms suited to the metric.

Counterweight: Do not create meeting overload.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17

## GC-102 — Document enough to transfer capability

Rule: Institutional knowledge should survive turnover and acquisition.

In practice: Maintain SOPs, reporting definitions and role playbooks for critical processes.

Counterweight: Documentation should focus on high-value processes.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20
