# Finance & Capital Allocation — 12 principles

## GC-057 — Prioritize income generation

Rule: Income expands strategic options.

In practice: When finances tighten, pair cost control with explicit income-growth initiatives.

Counterweight: Revenue without margin or cash conversion is insufficient.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S9, S8

## GC-058 — Prefer cash flow to idle cash

Rule: Capital should ultimately produce income or strategic capacity.

In practice: Separate operating liquidity from investable capital and define deployment thresholds.

Counterweight: Maintain adequate reserves.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S23, S22

## GC-059 — Maintain serious emergency reserves

Rule: Aggressive growth still requires resilience.

In practice: Define liquidity buffers sufficient for severe operating stress.

Counterweight: Reserve sizing should fit volatility and access to credit, not a universal number.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S22

## GC-060 — Distinguish productive from consumptive debt

Rule: Debt should create more income/value than it costs.

In practice: For each borrowing, identify the cash flow or asset value that services it.

Counterweight: Even productive debt can be excessive if refinancing or covenant risk is high.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S21, S22

## GC-061 — Use leverage to increase equity productivity

Rule: Borrowing can amplify returns when assets produce dependable cash.

In practice: Compare levered and unlevered returns plus stress cases.

Counterweight: Never judge leverage only on base-case returns.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S21, S24, S25

## GC-062 — Create dedicated investment capacity

Rule: Capital earmarked for future opportunities increases readiness.

In practice: Maintain investment accounts/funds separate from operating requirements.

Counterweight: Do not let earmarking override liquidity needs.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S22

## GC-063 — Evaluate every dollar by return

Rule: Capital, hires and major decisions should be assessed for return.

In practice: Use ROIC, payback, cash yield or enterprise-value impact as appropriate.

Counterweight: Some compliance, safety and reputation spending is mandatory despite indirect ROI.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S30, S17

## GC-064 — Do not worship budgets

Rule: A budget is a constraint tool; a financial plan should create growth.

In practice: Use budgets to control waste while preserving high-return growth spending.

Counterweight: Cash discipline remains essential.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S21, S22

## GC-065 — Track cash conversion, not just accounting profit

Rule: Reported earnings can hide working-capital stress.

In practice: Review free cash flow, receivables, inventory, capex and debt service.

Counterweight: Metrics vary by industry.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S23

## GC-066 — Price capital explicitly

Rule: Every financing source has cost, control and risk.

In practice: Compare debt, equity, seller financing, retained earnings and partnership capital.

Counterweight: Do not select nominally cheap capital with dangerous terms.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-067 — Keep dry powder for exceptional opportunities

Rule: Optional liquidity has strategic value.

In practice: Avoid fully deploying capital if it destroys ability to act during dislocation.

Counterweight: Excessive idle capital erodes returns.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S22, S28

## GC-068 — Tie growth spending to milestones

Rule: Aggressive investment needs stop-loss logic.

In practice: Define expected output, leading indicators, review dates and cut/scale thresholds.

Counterweight: Early experiments may require learning before financial proof.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S18
