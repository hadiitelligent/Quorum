# Crisis, Downturns & Adversity — 10 principles

## GC-113 — Do not disappear in a downturn

Rule: Weak markets can reward visible, active operators.

In practice: Maintain customer contact, sales activity and opportunity scanning.

Counterweight: Preserve cash if demand collapses structurally.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S28, S29

## GC-114 — Look for opportunity inside dislocation

Rule: Crises reprice assets, talent and market share.

In practice: Create a downturn opportunity list for acquisitions, hires, leases and marketing.

Counterweight: Do not assume every distressed asset is cheap.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S28

## GC-115 — Protect liquidity first enough to stay in the game

Rule: Survival preserves future upside.

In practice: Stress cash runway, covenants and refinancing early.

Counterweight: Avoid panic hoarding that blocks high-return opportunities.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S22, S28

## GC-116 — Increase sales intensity when demand weakens

Rule: Lower inbound demand often requires higher outbound activity.

In practice: Raise prospecting, follow-up and conversion discipline.

Counterweight: Do not overspend on channels with broken unit economics.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S8, S28

## GC-117 — Adapt the offer to changed conditions

Rule: A market shift may require new packaging, terms or channels.

In practice: Reassess customer pain, pricing and delivery model.

Counterweight: Do not abandon strategic coherence for every short-term trend.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S29, S28

## GC-118 — Reposition liabilities into assets where possible

Rule: Underused resources can sometimes be redeployed.

In practice: Identify idle space, staff capacity, inventory, equipment or contracts that can generate value.

Counterweight: Avoid accounting gimmicks or hidden risk.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S28

## GC-119 — Keep training during contraction

Rule: Skill improvement can increase share while competitors retreat.

In practice: Use slow periods to improve sales, management and systems.

Counterweight: Training does not replace necessary restructuring.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S28, S14

## GC-120 — Make decisions before the crisis makes them for you

Rule: Early action preserves options.

In practice: Set triggers for liquidity, hiring, capex and debt actions.

Counterweight: Avoid overreacting to short-lived volatility.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S22, S28

## GC-121 — Use severe scenarios, not only forecasts

Rule: Crises expose optimism bias.

In practice: Run downside cases beyond management's expected case.

Counterweight: Scenarios should be plausible, not theatrical.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S18, S26

## GC-122 — Exit permanently impaired theses

Rule: Persistence should not become denial.

In practice: If customer behavior, regulation or economics have structurally changed, reallocate.

Counterweight: Require evidence before declaring permanent impairment.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S29, S18
