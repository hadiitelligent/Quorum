# Partnerships, M&A & Network — 10 principles

## GC-143 — Relationships expand deal flow

Rule: Access to lenders, operators and investors creates advantage.

In practice: Invest in high-quality networks before a transaction is needed.

Counterweight: Do not substitute relationships for diligence.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-144 — Prepare the business before acquiring

Rule: Acquisition capacity requires clean financials and management systems.

In practice: Standardize P&L, processes, banking and leadership before pursuing a roll-up.

Counterweight: Opportunistic acquisitions may precede perfect readiness.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S19

## GC-145 — Use bolt-ons to scale proven platforms

Rule: Acquisitions can accelerate market share when the integration model exists.

In practice: Favor targets that fit a repeatable integration thesis.

Counterweight: Do not acquire merely to increase revenue.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S20

## GC-146 — Build a leadership bench before integration

Rule: Acquisitions fail when all decisions bottleneck at the founder.

In practice: Identify integration leaders and functional owners pre-close.

Counterweight: Use interim support when permanent hires are not justified.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-147 — Model post-close execution before signing

Rule: The deal is not complete at LOI or close.

In practice: Build 30/60/90-day integration plans with owners and KPIs.

Counterweight: Preserve flexibility for new information after close.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S20

## GC-148 — Choose partners who add leverage

Rule: Partnerships should contribute capital, distribution, expertise, credibility or execution.

In practice: Quantify the partner's unique contribution and dependency risk.

Counterweight: Avoid partnerships based solely on prestige.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S19

## GC-149 — Protect control where it affects execution

Rule: Capital can be expensive if governance blocks the strategy.

In practice: Evaluate voting, veto, board and information rights.

Counterweight: Shared control can improve governance and reduce founder risk.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-150 — Use banking relationships as strategic infrastructure

Rule: Financing readiness can improve acquisition speed and credibility.

In practice: Maintain lender relationships and current reporting before deals arise.

Counterweight: Do not become captive to one lender.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S19

## GC-151 — Acquire systems and talent, not just revenue

Rule: Strategic buyers should value capabilities that improve the platform.

In practice: Assess management, customer relationships, processes and data in diligence.

Counterweight: Do not pay for capabilities that cannot be retained.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-152 — Design the exit before entry

Rule: Acquisition theses should include plausible future owners or monetization paths.

In practice: Model hold, recap, refinance and sale alternatives.

Counterweight: Do not optimize short-term exit at the expense of compounding value.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S24
