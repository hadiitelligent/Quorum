# Real Estate Investing — 12 principles

## GC-069 — Favor income-producing assets

Rule: Cash-producing property is preferable to pure appreciation speculation.

In practice: Prioritize assets with credible current or near-term positive cash flow.

Counterweight: Value-add assets may tolerate lower early cash flow when the repositioning thesis is strong.

## GC-070 — Favor scale in multifamily

Rule: Larger multifamily properties fit the documented institutional strategy.

In practice: When feasible, compare 200+ unit opportunities with smaller assets on management efficiency and financing.

Counterweight: Do not force unit count when local economics favor another format.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S25

## GC-071 — Prefer quality locations

Rule: Location quality is a core underwriting input.

In practice: Score employment, demographics, supply, demand, access and tenant quality.

Counterweight: A great location does not justify any price.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S24, S26

## GC-072 — Seek purchase-price advantage

Rule: Buying below replacement cost can improve downside protection.

In practice: Compare purchase basis with current replacement economics where relevant.

Counterweight: Replacement cost is not a complete valuation method.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S26

## GC-073 — Underwrite occupancy quality

Rule: Existing occupancy provides evidence of demand and cash flow.

In practice: Stress rent roll, concessions, delinquency and renewal data rather than headline occupancy alone.

Counterweight: Value-add/lease-up deals require separate treatment.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S25

## GC-074 — Use leverage within survivable ranges

Rule: Institutional filings commonly contemplate meaningful leverage.

In practice: Model 60-80% leverage only where debt service and refinance risk remain acceptable.

Counterweight: Do not treat historical target ranges as universal mandates.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S25, S24

## GC-075 — Target controllable NOI growth

Rule: Operational improvement is preferable to relying solely on cap-rate compression.

In practice: Identify rent, expense, ancillary revenue and management levers.

Counterweight: NOI assumptions must respect market and tenant constraints.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S24, S26

## GC-076 — Accept moderate value-add when economics justify it

Rule: Repositioning can improve returns.

In practice: Price capex, downtime, execution risk and stabilization timing explicitly.

Counterweight: Avoid vague renovation premiums.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S25

## GC-077 — Use third-party management as a scaling tool

Rule: Property operations can be delegated while ownership controls strategy.

In practice: Select management based on KPIs, reporting and local execution.

Counterweight: Retain oversight of budgets, leasing, capex and compliance.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S24, S26

## GC-078 — Think in long holding periods

Rule: Documented fund structures contemplate multi-year holds.

In practice: Underwrite cycles, debt maturities and capex over 7-10 year-like horizons when appropriate.

Counterweight: Exit earlier when risk-adjusted returns or capital recycling justify it.

Evidence A · aggression 8/10 · sources S24

## GC-079 — Preserve multiple exits

Rule: Assets should ideally support refinance, sale or continued hold.

In practice: Model exit alternatives before acquisition.

Counterweight: Do not rely on refinancing being available.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S24, S25

## GC-080 — Underwrite the downside before celebrating leverage

Rule: Real estate debt magnifies errors.

In practice: Stress occupancy, rates, expenses, cap rates and capex simultaneously.

Counterweight: Aggressive acquisition requires stronger—not weaker—stress testing.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S24, S25, S26
