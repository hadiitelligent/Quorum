# Meta Decision Patterns — 10 principles

## GC-163 — Ask whether the question is too small

Rule: Management may frame a tactical question when the real issue is strategic.

In practice: Before answering, restate the highest-value version of the question.

Counterweight: Still answer the original operational need.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S1, S2

## GC-164 — Prefer controllable upside

Rule: Growth is higher quality when execution affects the result.

In practice: Score controllability in every major investment.

Counterweight: External tailwinds can still be valuable.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S24, S26

## GC-165 — Prefer action with reversibility

Rule: Fast moves are attractive when downside can be capped.

In practice: Distinguish reversible experiments from irreversible commitments.

Counterweight: Do not delay reversible tests for false certainty.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S4, S17

## GC-166 — Use asymmetric experiments

Rule: Test big ideas cheaply before full deployment when possible.

In practice: Pilot channels, offers, geographies or systems with explicit scale criteria.

Counterweight: Some opportunities require commitment before perfect testing.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S4, S17

## GC-167 — Scale winners aggressively

Rule: Once unit economics and execution are proven, underinvestment becomes a strategic error.

In practice: Increase capital and capacity behind validated growth engines.

Counterweight: Watch for saturation and diminishing returns.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S17, S30

## GC-168 — Kill weak initiatives faster

Rule: Persistence applies to worthy goals, not every tactic.

In practice: Stop tactics that repeatedly fail pre-agreed evidence thresholds.

Counterweight: Ensure the test was adequately powered and executed.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S3, S17

## GC-169 — Convert uncertainty into terms

Rule: When risk is material but not fatal, restructure rather than defaulting to pass.

In practice: Use contingencies, staged funding, earnouts, covenants or options.

Counterweight: Some risks cannot be contracted away.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S19, S20

## GC-170 — Ask who benefits and who bears downside

Rule: Strong decisions account for stakeholder asymmetry.

In practice: Map economics and risk across owners, employees, customers, lenders and partners.

Counterweight: Do not require perfectly equal outcomes.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S11, S18

## GC-171 — Protect the right to act tomorrow

Rule: Today's decision should not unnecessarily destroy future strategic capacity.

In practice: Measure remaining liquidity, management bandwidth and covenant headroom after the decision.

Counterweight: Occasionally concentration is justified by an exceptional opportunity.

Evidence C · aggression 8/10 · sources S22, S30

## GC-172 — End with a concrete next move

Rule: Analysis without action is incomplete.

In practice: Every board review concludes with owner, action, deadline and success criterion.

Counterweight: Where information is genuinely insufficient, the next move can be targeted diligence.

Evidence B · aggression 8/10 · sources S14, S17
